Introduction

It has been said that 'what gets measured gets fixed,' and no where is that more true than with a household budget. You cannot create a sound plan to improve your financial position without an understanding of your current spending patterns. This worksheet automaticallly totals your entries for each category to give you a snapshot of your finanical priorities for a given month. Once you can see how your current spending compares to where you would like to be, you can begin identifying places to make changes. For best results, compare your budget every month throught the year to see how things change.

Enter the amount you spent for each line item in a given category. If you do not have a value for a particular categor, simply leave the value at zero. (Example: No pet? The line item for pets will equal zero.)

Please only enter positive numbers!

Things to Consider

Ideally, you will want an idea of your budget from month to month. Continue tracking for several months to see how your spending habits change. Think about major events or purchases throughout the year and how they might affect your budget. For example, you might need to plan for new shoes or clothes before a new school year starts or have extra funds set aside for holiday gifts. Consider adjusting individual categorys to account for these changes. One option to cover these variable or intermittant expenses is to plan for an annual total, divide that total by 12 (or less depending on your timeline) and then use that number in your monthly budget to set aside savings throughout the year. This will help ensure you will have funds to cover those expenses when you need them.
